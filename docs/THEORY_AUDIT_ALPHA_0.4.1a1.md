# Alpha 0.4.1a1 — Phase C.1 theory completeness, scientific and bilingual editorial audit

Status: pre-Phase-D audit. This document records the review performed after Phase C was merged.

This audit changes explanatory content and metadata only. It does **not** add a new executable mechanism, equation, coefficient, empirical calibration, population estimate, ranking engine, social network, AI agent, stress transition or numerical Reflective Distance state.

## 1. Audit question

The review asked whether the Theory surface is sufficiently:

- complete relative to CEM's own twenty-module scientific architecture;
- detailed enough to explain the logic behind the executable M0/M1 slice;
- explicit about empirical, executable, conceptual and interpretive status;
- argued from appropriate primary studies, systematic reviews or established theoretical sources;
- internally coherent and causally staged;
- semantically aligned between English and Romanian;
- written in natural Romanian rather than literal or mechanically translated language.

The full corpus reviewed was 16 English chapters + 16 Romanian chapters, together with `theory_index.json`, `theory_glossary.json`, the 20-module map, model evidence registry, Alpha 0.4 scope and Theory/Guided Understanding planning contract.

## 2. Main finding before revision

The Phase C corpus was scientifically cautious and correctly separated executable from conceptual material, but it was not yet fully explanatory relative to the project's own architecture.

The main coverage gaps were:

- MOD.08 — population and network heterogeneity;
- MOD.10 — cross-platform information ecosystems;
- MOD.11 — human–AI epistemic intermediation;
- MOD.12 — delegation and appropriate reliance;
- MOD.13 — skill acquisition, deskilling and human oversight;
- MOD.15 — heuristic policy selection;
- MOD.19 — strategic influence / adversarial production;
- MOD.20 — epistemic institutions and authority.

The Romanian corpus also contained too many literal English imports for a canonical reader: `baseline`, `ground truth`, `pool`, `ranking`, `nested null`, `pattern`, `planner`, `task-specific`, `accuracy cue`, `outcome`, `sharing`, `fitness for purpose` and similar constructions.

## 3. Scientific revisions

### Chapter 0 — scope and scientific map

The chapter now explains explicitly that the twenty modules are a scientific map, not twenty finished software engines. It distinguishes the broader subsystems from the smaller executable slice and states which important mechanisms remain conceptual.

### Chapter 2 — heuristics and metacontrol

The earlier Type 1 / Type 2 discussion was not sufficient for MOD.15. The chapter now treats heuristics as task- and environment-dependent policies rather than synonyms for bias or irrationality. It introduces ecological rationality and states what would be required before a heuristic selector could become executable.

Key background:
- Gigerenzer & Gaissmaier (2011), *Heuristic Decision Making*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-120709-145346
- Evans & Stanovich (2013), *Dual-Process Theories of Higher Cognition*: https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691612460685

### Chapter 3 — need for cognitive closure

The Romanian text now uses `nevoie de închidere cognitivă` as the principal term, with `seizing` and `freezing` retained only as established labels from the original theory.

Key background:
- Kruglanski & Webster (1996): https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.2.263

### Chapter 4 — stress and resources

The chapter keeps stress at the phenomenon level and explicitly avoids a global “prefrontal shutdown” narrative or a numerical stress slider.

Key background:
- Shields, Sazma & Yonelinas (2016): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003767/

### Chapter 7 — sources and epistemic authority

The chapter now separates:
1. actual source performance;
2. the agent's estimate of source reliability;
3. truth of the current claim;
4. broader institutional authority.

MOD.20 is now explained as a conceptual layer rather than being implicitly compressed into `T`.

Key background:
- Sanna & Lagnado (2025): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106090
- Bor et al. / social-cue evidence registered for the broader social-source context: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54030-y

### Chapter 9 — editorial media system

M1.E1 is now placed inside a broader media theory that distinguishes editorial selection, gatekeeping, agenda setting, broader framing, repetition and platform redistribution. Television and online news are described as different information channels rather than interchangeable media surfaces.

Background used:
- News Editing and Editorial Process: https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780190228613.013.802
- Gatekeeping and Journalism: https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780190228613.013.819
- Television News: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-publhealth-040218-044017
- Incidental news exposure scoping review: https://doi.org/10.1080/23808985.2023.2169953

### Chapter 10 — congruence, identity and motivated reasoning

The chapter now makes explicit that `Gatt` is a task-specific relation and must not be reinterpreted as ideology, partisan identity or a general confirmation-bias trait. MOD.05 is linked conceptually, not numerically.

Background:
- *Partisan Bias in Political Judgment* (Annual Review of Psychology): https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-030424-122723

### Chapter 11 — information ecosystem

This is the largest completeness revision. The chapter now separates:

`production → editorial availability → platform ranking → exposure → attention/processing → internal representation → judgment → action → social/platform feedback → later exposure`

It provides explicit explanatory homes for:

- MOD.08 population/network heterogeneity;
- MOD.10 cross-platform adaptation;
- MOD.11 human–AI epistemic intermediation;
- MOD.12 appropriate reliance;
- MOD.13 skill change / deskilling / oversight;
- MOD.18 social evidence;
- MOD.19 strategic influence.

The text does not make these modules executable.

Background added:
- social cues and perceived consensus: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54030-y
- incidental / cross-platform news contact: https://doi.org/10.1080/23808985.2023.2169953
- AI advice review: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1390182
- Lee et al. (CHI 2025), GenAI and critical thinking: https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778
- healthcare AI deskilling / oversight review: https://doi.org/10.2147/JHL.S590498

### Chapter 12 — interventions

The chapter now distinguishes individual-level and ecosystem-level intervention locations, adds explicit warnings against summing effect sizes from unlike studies, and treats the planner as a scenario laboratory rather than a policy recommender.

### Chapter 13 — individuation

Jungian individuation remains INTERPRETIVE. Reflective Distance is treated as a separate CONCEPTUAL bridge to metacognition and decentering. The bilingual texts now state explicitly what operationalization would be required before numerical modelling.

### Chapter 14 — validation

The validation chapter was expanded substantially. It now distinguishes:

- software verification from scientific validation;
- pattern targets from parameters;
- nested nulls from causal proof;
- calibration from demonstration;
- practical from structural identifiability;
- sensitivity from uncertainty;
- internal from external validity;
- triangulation from simple study counting;
- documentation/provenance from empirical validation;
- general validity claims from fitness for purpose.

Background:
- Grimm et al. (2020), updated ODD protocol: https://www.jasss.org/23/2/7.html

## 4. Romanian editorial standard

The Romanian version is an adaptation, not a word-for-word translation.

Preferred canonical forms include:

| Mechanical / English-first form | Canonical Romanian form |
|---|---|
| ground truth | adevărul de referință al simulării |
| baseline | model / valoare de referință |
| pool | set de informații |
| ranking | ordonare algoritmică |
| nested null | model nul imbricat |
| pattern | tipar |
| planner | modul de planificare |
| task-specific | specific sarcinii |
| accuracy cue / prompt | indiciu de orientare către acuratețe |
| sharing | distribuire |
| outcome | rezultat |
| fitness for purpose | adecvare la scop |
| predictive coding | codare predictivă (predictive coding) |
| need for closure | nevoie de închidere cognitivă |

English terms are retained when they are:
- code identifiers or equation symbols;
- canonical model names (`NULL`, `FRAME_ONLY`, `FRAME_CONGRUENCE`);
- established literature terms that benefit from one parenthetical English form (`framing`, `prebunking`, `seizing`, `freezing`);
- document/status identifiers such as `MODEL_EVIDENCE`, `BACKGROUND_THEORY`, `INTERPRETIVE_SOURCE`.

## 5. Completeness gate added to CI

The theory test suite now requires the union of module IDs covered by the 16 Theory chapters to equal the complete module registry.

This is an explanatory-completeness test, **not** an execution test. A conceptual module can satisfy the Theory coverage gate while remaining non-executable.

This prevents the scientific architecture from silently expanding beyond what the canonical reader explains.

## 6. Epistemic boundaries retained

The audit deliberately does not add:

- Track A/B population prevalence;
- individual diagnosis;
- stress coefficients;
- a heuristic-selector equation;
- a platform recommender;
- social-network diffusion;
- a human–AI numerical mechanism;
- a deskilling coefficient;
- an institutional-authority score;
- an adversarial-producer engine;
- a numerical Reflective Distance state;
- new intervention calibration.

Published effect sizes remain evidence or validation context unless a future calibration protocol explicitly maps them to simulator observables.

## 7. Editorial conclusion

After these revisions, the theory corpus is much closer to the intended role of a self-contained explanatory layer: a reader should be able to understand not only the currently executable mechanisms, but also where the major conceptual modules belong, why they are not yet numerical, what evidence motivates them, and what would be required to test them.

The remaining next step is Phase D — Guided Tour. It should be built *from* this audited theory and the existing executable scenarios, not used to compensate for gaps in the theory.
