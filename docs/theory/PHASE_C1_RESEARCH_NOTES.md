# Phase C.1 research notes

These notes record why the theory corpus was expanded before the Guided Tour.

## Repetition and judged truth

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis covers 182 studies and 366 effect sizes and reports a small average illusory-truth effect after adjustment for small-study effects, with substantial heterogeneity. A separate 2026 study found no reliable repetition effect for social-political evaluative opinion statements in two preregistered experiments. The CEM repetition chapter therefore treats repetition as a bounded phenomenon rather than a universal law.

## Algorithmic feeds

A 2026 randomized field experiment on X found that switching users from chronological to algorithmic feeds for seven weeks increased engagement and shifted several measured political attitudes in the direction of content promoted by the feed, while not significantly changing affective polarization or self-reported partisanship. Earlier Meta-platform experiments did not show the same downstream attitude effects. CEM therefore keeps ranking, exposure, processing, belief and action as separate stages.

## Exposure heterogeneity

A 2024 Nature perspective synthesizing behavioural evidence argues that average exposure to false and inflammatory content online is often low and concentrated among a narrow subset of users. This motivates explicit future treatment of population and network heterogeneity rather than treating a population mean as representative of every agent.

## Human–automation reliance

Human-factors research distinguishes trust in automation from actual reliance and documents automation-bias/overreliance risks. CEM therefore reserves separate constructs for perceived reliability, actual performance, verification cost and the accept/check/override decision.

## Editorial implication

The research above changes the presentation requirements of the theory corpus. A scientifically responsible Theory Reader should repeatedly expose scope conditions, competing explanations and the difference between a phenomenon-level result and a calibrated executable mechanism. Phase C.1 implements that editorial rule.
