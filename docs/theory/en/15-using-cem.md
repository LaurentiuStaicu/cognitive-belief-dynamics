# How to use CEM without confusing the map with the territory

## Central idea

CEM is most useful when explanation comes before optimization:

Understand → explore the mechanism → inspect evidence → compare factors → plan action.

[[VIEW:learning]] · [[VIEW:reference]] · [[VIEW:planning]] · [[VIEW:process]]

## 1. Build the conceptual map first

Start in Theory. Chapters 0–4 explain the architecture and broad cognitive background. Chapters 5–10 connect empirically anchored phenomena to the currently executable M0/M1 mechanisms. Chapter 11 widens the view to networks, platforms, social evidence, AI intermediation and strategic influence while keeping those layers conceptual. Chapter 12 locates interventions in the causal chain. Chapter 13 preserves the interpretive individuation layer. Chapter 14 explains validation.

When you encounter an interactive term, select it. The inspector should answer at least three questions: what does it mean, what is its epistemic status, and where does it appear in the model?

A fourth question is often equally important: **what does it explicitly not mean?**

## 2. Trace one mechanism end to end

Use [[VIEW:learning]] for mechanisms and reference scenarios. Follow intermediate variables rather than jumping directly to the final result.

For repetition, trace exposure → familiarity → belief contribution. For correction, trace correction event → corrective accessibility → later belief contribution. For source learning, separate source performance from the agent's estimate T. For M1.E1, keep the available information set fixed while selection changes. For M1.E2, keep semantic meaning fixed while presentation changes.

If you cannot say what changed and what remained invariant, do not interpret the final difference yet.

## 3. Inspect implementation and evidence separately

Open [[VIEW:reference]]. Check whether a source is MODEL_EVIDENCE, BACKGROUND_THEORY or INTERPRETIVE_SOURCE. A paper cited near an equation does not automatically estimate that equation.

Check the Registry's “what it is not” statements as carefully as its definitions. These boundaries prevent category errors such as treating familiarity as truth, W as intelligence, T as objective source quality, Gatt as political identity, or engagement as belief.

For process ordering and implementation structure, use [[VIEW:process]]. Visual ODD answers “how does the simulator run?” while Theory answers “why is the distinction made and what supports it?”

## 4. Distinguish current mechanisms from future architecture

The twenty-module map is broader than the executable model. Chapters 2, 7 and 11 deliberately expose important conceptual layers such as heuristic policy selection, institutional authority, network heterogeneity, AI intermediation and strategic influence.

Do not assign these modules numerical effects merely because they appear in the map. Their presence means “this factor belongs in the scientific architecture and requires explicit future testing”, not “this factor is already quantified”.

## 5. Compare factors under declared assumptions

Use published scenarios to see how a controlled change affects a trajectory. Use nested models where they exist. Compare intermediate states, not only final scores.

A difference in a synthetic run is a **model consequence under declared assumptions**. It is not automatically a percentage effect in a real population.

When a result depends strongly on a parameter, cost or scenario choice, sensitivity is part of the conclusion. It should not be hidden.

## 6. Plan action only after understanding the mechanisms

Only then open [[VIEW:planning]]. Choose the objective, budget and effort costs, and inspect why one combination is ranked above another. Look at leave-one-out comparisons, non-additivity and sensitivity profiles.

The result is a priority **inside the current model**. It is not an automatic policy recommendation. A real intervention decision also requires feasibility, reach, equity, implementation capacity, adverse effects, uncertainty and monitoring.

## 7. Ask the epistemic control question

On any screen ask:

**Am I looking at an empirical finding, an executable model component, a conceptual hypothesis, or an interpretation?**

Then ask:

**What evidence would make me revise this part of the model?**

If either answer is unclear, return to the Registry, Theory and validation chapter.

## 8. Use the model iteratively

A good use of CEM is cyclical rather than linear:

theory → mechanism → scenario → discrepancy → evidence → model revision → new test.

The objective is not to make every observation fit. The objective is to expose assumptions clearly enough that some of them can fail.

## What this chapter does not claim

Correct use of the interface does not remove scientific uncertainty. CEM is a tool for explaining, comparing and testing candidate mechanisms. Its value increases when limitations, rejected explanations and unresolved questions are as inspectable as successful model outputs.
