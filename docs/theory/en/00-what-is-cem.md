# What is the Cognitive Epistemic Model?

> Phase C · Explanatory theory. CEM explicitly separates empirical evidence, executable mechanisms, and conceptual or interpretive layers.

## Central idea

The Cognitive Epistemic Model, or CEM, is a computational and conceptual model for studying the path by which information available to an agent can become observed information, an internal representation, a judgment, and an action. Its central question is not “why are people rational or irrational?” but a more precise one: which stages and mechanisms can alter what reaches an agent, which internal states are updated, how a judgment is formed, and under what conditions that judgment does or does not become action?

This separation matters because the world, information about the world, and an internal model of the world are not the same thing. An agent may receive only part of the available information; two fact-compatible presentations can emphasize different subsets; a repeated claim can become more familiar without becoming more true; and a person can judge something likely true without choosing to share it. CEM represents these distinctions rather than compressing them into a generic “critical thinking” score.

## What is executable now

M0 is the retained executable baseline. It contains candidate mechanisms for exposure and familiarity, corrective-context accessibility, estimated source reliability, belief formation, and sharing decisions. M0 is deliberately designed so that simulated ground truth is not passed directly into the function that updates belief. The agent receives events and signals, not privileged access to truth.

M1.E1 adds an executable distinction between an available factual pool and an editorially selected subset. M1.E2 adds a comparison of semantically equivalent confirmation and refutation presentations and a demonstrative interaction with task-specific attitudinal congruence. Both are candidate extensions, not calibrated population models.

Current coefficients are demonstrative reference values. They make mechanisms and differential predictions inspectable in synthetic scenarios. Published empirical effects are validation targets or context; they are not silently converted into CEM parameters.

## How to read epistemic status

EMPIRICAL means that empirical evidence supports the phenomenon at the stated level. It does not mean that the exact CEM equation was estimated from those data. EXECUTABLE means that the construct or relation exists in the simulator and has a canonical implementation. CONCEPTUAL means that the idea belongs to the explanatory architecture but is not yet a validated numerical mechanism. INTERPRETIVE marks philosophical or integrative material rather than calibration evidence.

Statuses may coexist. A phenomenon can be empirical while the CEM function representing it remains only an executable candidate form.

## What problem the model addresses

CEM has three ordered goals. First is mechanism understanding: variables, factors, interactions and limits. Second is factor comparison within defined scenarios. Third is planning combinations of interventions inside the model. The ordering matters: a numerical ranking without understanding the causal chain can create false precision.

ODD and the Registry formally describe the model. Theory explains why distinctions are made and what research supports them. Scenarios expose mechanism behavior. The planner compares options only under its declared assumptions. None of these surfaces should be mistaken for a direct estimate of social reality.

## What this chapter does not claim

CEM does not estimate the share of a population in “Track A” or “Track B”, diagnose individuals, automatically assign cognitive errors to groups, or provide quantitative forecasts for Romania or elections. It does not claim that all twenty conceptual modules are executable. It also does not claim that reproducing several patterns proves the model true; pattern reproduction is a testing condition, not universal validation.
