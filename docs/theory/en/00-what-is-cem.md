# What is the Cognitive Epistemic Model?

> Phase C.1 · Explanatory theory. CEM separates empirical evidence, executable mechanisms, conceptual hypotheses and interpretive material, so that a useful explanation is not mistaken for a calibrated fact.

## Central idea

The Cognitive Epistemic Model, or CEM, is a computational and conceptual framework for studying the path by which information available to an agent can become observed information, an internal representation, a judgment and an action. Its central question is not “why are people rational or irrational?” but a more precise one: which stages and mechanisms can alter what reaches an agent, which internal states change, how a judgment is formed, and under what conditions that judgment does or does not become action?

This separation matters because the world, information about the world and a person's internal representation of the world are not the same thing. An agent may receive only part of the available information; two fact-compatible presentations can emphasize different subsets; a repeated claim can become more familiar without becoming more true; and a person can judge something likely true without choosing to share it. CEM represents these distinctions rather than compressing them into a single “critical thinking” score.

## The scientific map and the executable model are different things

CEM currently has a twenty-module scientific map. The modules organize questions about state conditions, epistemic processing, information environments, memory, social identity, affect, action, population and network heterogeneity, attention, cross-platform adaptation, human–AI intermediation, reliance on AI, skill change and oversight, world-model construction, heuristic policy selection, editorial media, self-integration, social evidence, strategic influence and epistemic institutions.

The twenty modules are **not** twenty finished software engines. They are a map of the problem space. Only a smaller set of relations is executable in the current simulator. This distinction is intentional: a concept enters the numerical model only when it can be operationalized, linked to observable evidence, given a differential prediction and exposed to a result that could count against it.

The modules are also grouped into broader subsystems: world/information, human agent, sources/institutions, media/strategic actors, platform/network, AI system, learning/adaptation and observation/validation. These groupings help the reader see where a future mechanism would belong without pretending that the mechanism already exists.

## What is executable now

M0 is the retained executable baseline. It contains candidate mechanisms for exposure and familiarity, corrective-context accessibility, estimated source reliability, belief formation and sharing decisions. M0 is deliberately designed so that simulated ground truth is not passed directly into the function that updates belief. The agent receives events and signals, not privileged access to truth.

M1.E1 adds an executable distinction between an available fact-compatible information set and an editorially selected subset. M1.E2 compares semantically equivalent confirmation and refutation presentations and includes a demonstrative interaction with task-specific attitudinal congruence. Both are candidate extensions, not calibrated population models.

Current coefficients are demonstrative reference values. They make mechanisms and differential predictions inspectable in synthetic scenarios. Published empirical effects are validation targets or theoretical constraints; they are not silently converted into CEM parameters.

## What remains conceptual or interpretive

Several important parts of the scientific map are not yet executable. These include a general heuristic selector, a recommender-system model, network diffusion and population heterogeneity, cross-platform adaptation, human–AI delegation and deskilling, strategic influence production, an explicit model of epistemic institutions, stress-driven transitions and a numerical measure of reflective distance.

Keeping these layers visible is useful because it prevents the executable slice from being mistaken for a complete theory of cognition or information ecosystems. Keeping them non-numerical is equally important because assigning values before valid operationalization would create false precision.

Jungian individuation belongs to a separate interpretive layer. It can motivate questions about self-integration and epistemic autonomy, but it is not treated as a validated neurocognitive mechanism.

## How to read epistemic status

EMPIRICAL means that empirical evidence supports the phenomenon at the stated level. It does not mean that the exact CEM equation was estimated from those data. EXECUTABLE means that the construct or relation exists in the simulator and has a canonical implementation. CONCEPTUAL means that the idea belongs to the explanatory architecture but is not yet a validated numerical mechanism. INTERPRETIVE marks philosophical, clinical-historical or integrative material rather than calibration evidence.

Statuses may coexist. A phenomenon can be empirically supported while the CEM function representing it remains only an executable candidate form.

## What problem the model addresses

CEM has three ordered goals. First is mechanism understanding: variables, factors, interactions, assumptions and limits. Second is factor comparison within defined scenarios. Third is planning combinations of interventions inside the model. The ordering matters. A numerical ranking without understanding the causal chain can create an impression of precision that the evidence does not support.

ODD and the Registry formally describe the model. Theory explains why distinctions are made and what research supports them. Scenarios expose mechanism behavior. The planner compares options only under its declared assumptions. None of these surfaces should be mistaken for a direct estimate of social reality.

## What this chapter does not claim

CEM does not estimate the share of a population in “Track A” or “Track B”, diagnose individuals, automatically assign cognitive errors to groups, or provide quantitative forecasts for Romania, elections or political behavior. It does not claim that all twenty conceptual modules are executable. It does not claim that reproducing several patterns proves the model true: pattern reproduction is one test condition, not universal validation.

## Evidence and epistemic status

Methodological background: the ODD protocol developed by Grimm and colleagues structures simulation-model descriptions for clarity, replication and fitness-for-purpose evaluation. CEM complements ODD with explanatory theory, evidence roles and explicit scope limits. None of these layers substitutes for empirical validation.
